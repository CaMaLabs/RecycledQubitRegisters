Secondary FQ+Brent portfolio gate: PASS (13/24 vs Brent; 13/24 required), but gains are concentrated in the synthetically created near and middle strata, not the far stratum.** This is a correctly recovered, bounded, classical algorithm experiment—NOT an asymptotic factorization/quantum win. **Zero IBM QPU, zero paid/free IBM Runtime.**

## Fixed methods and resource accounting

All complete factoring methods start from public N, test small primes, and use identical deterministic optimized Brent budget c1 seed2 max1024 modular squarings, then c3 seed3 max512; when necessary, an identical full wheel210 fallback ensures complete factor recovery on the synthetic benchmark. The independent B baseline is that Brent-first path. F prepends 8 plain Fermat square roots. Q prepends the safe Dark Star original `M=255255` quadratic-residue midpoint sieve with 2048 candidate positions and ≤96 exact square roots (plus all modular checks and per-run mask setup). FQ prepends both Fermat-8 and Q before the same Brent/fallback. Independent full wheel210 and PTP LFK factor all the same N; a 2048-position plain Fermat control may remain unresolved. All four methods B,F,Q,FQ are complete, and **all 24 correct factors were verified under B,F,Q,FQ, wheel210 and LFK**. There is no secret factor/order/stratum in any algorithm's function arguments.

**PRIMARY preregistered PTP-specific middle gate: FAILED.** Q needed to beat both B and F on >=6/8 original middle-stratum cases. It won **5/8 vs each**. Its median within-middle per-case runtime ratio Q/B was 0.7794 (~22% lower for those eight casewise median ratios), which is a useful *descriptive signal*, not a passed majority criterion or an algorithmic theorem.

**SECONDARY FQ broad portfolio gate: PASSED numerically 13/24 vs Brent (minimum 13)**. However, this threshold was met by **8 near-factor cases where the F early-Fermat branch found factors before Q was executed**, plus five middle-stratum Q wins, with **zero wins on far-factor cases**. Thus the 13/24 pass does **not** demonstrate that a public N-only selector can determine factor closeness or that PTP pruning is broadly faster than Brent. The near-factor advantage is a *standard plain Fermat* advantage, not a new Dark Star PTP advantage.

### Mechanism breakdown and negative findings

- **Near D=0..8:** Standalone Brent factors all cases, but the classic first 8 Fermat midpoint squares finish them in ~6.5–8.2 microseconds median (versus ~126–241 microseconds for Brent), without paying for PTP table/masks. Q is also faster than Brent because it finds the same midpoint within ≤8 x positions but takes ~32–37 microseconds including small mask/setup costs—**slower than simple Fermat F in 8/8**. FQ never even enters Q for these eight.
- **Middle D=64..640:** Q and FQ factor **all 8 directly using the safe PTP QR stage**, after scanning between 90 and 535 midpoint positions, requiring only **1–9 expensive exact integer-square-root tests** (not 90–535), and charging 143–1170 modular-membership checks plus setup. Q/B median ratio **0.7794** but Q loses on three cases where Brent's randomized-structure-equivalent deterministic trajectory finds the factor faster. The plain Fermat8 prefix cannot reach these midpoint offsets and usually pays overhead.
- **Far D=4096..16384:** All eight Q and FQ calls scan the full **2048-position cap** without finding the true midpoint, perform **~3200–5100 modular-member tests** and 15–73 exact square roots, then pay the full Brent factoring budget. Q/B median ratio **5.8292** (~5.8× slower at these tiny microsecond-scale timings), and Q loses all eight. No hidden stratum-based route switch was enabled to avoid this, correctly revealing the failure mode.
- **Both pass and fail outcomes preserved.** Any future design using the synthetic separation label to decide a method would leak construction truth and is invalid as a public unknown-semiprime router. A public-only early termination or distance estimate must be **proven independent of hidden p/q**, preregistered, and tested on an entirely different cohort.

## Interpretation

The original user-developed PTP midpoint pruning is mathematically correct and highly efficient **at rejecting Fermat midpoints and suppressing square-root calls**. This larger prospective test shows it can reduce total wall time over an optimized Brent baseline in *some explicitly selected intermediate-separation synthetic composites*, but its original primary strict advantage gate did not pass and there is **no public predictor yet that reliably identifies that group from an arbitrary N**. For close pairs, ordinary Fermat is better; for distant pairs, Brent is substantially better. Population strata were generated with known private synthetic p/q as experimental sampling only—one cannot assume an algorithm has those strata available for real unknown N.

The result is limited to 32-bit synthetic semiprimes, one deterministic rho polynomial schedule, small Python implementation microbenchmarks and nine timing repeats. It does not establish confidence intervals, native quantum benefit, real world RSA factoring, ML-KEM compromise, new asymptotic complexity, or speedup over best-in-class optimized arithmetic libraries. No actual QPU job was submitted.

**Next gate:** if continuing, the nontrivial research challenge is a *public-only*, low-cost predictor or adaptive online test that can choose among short Fermat, midrange PTP QR and Brent **without** using private p/q, synthetic stratum labels, or expensive leaked factorizations. Freeze a new N33/34 population and compare that new decision policy with optimized Brent and ordinary Fermat controls from the initial preregistration. Keep the PTP-specific 5/8 failure as part of the scientific record.
