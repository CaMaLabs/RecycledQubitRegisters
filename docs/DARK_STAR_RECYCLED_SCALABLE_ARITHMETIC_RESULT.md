# Dark Star + recycled-QPE scalable-arithmetic result

Date: 2026-10-14

## Scope

This result completes the next falsification gate after the small-N full-register
Dark Star compiler studies.

The audit combines:

- public residue-conditioned odd-power Shor preconditioning;
- staged/iterative phase estimation;
- one-qubit phase-register recycling; and
- the repository's generic reusable-clean-ancilla reversible modular-arithmetic
  construction.

Unlike the N=209/713/781 IBM-target scout compilers, this resource model does
**not** synthesize a truth table over all work-register basis states. It mirrors
the polynomial generic arithmetic in
`hardware/ibm_shor_generic_arithmetic_clean_mcx.py` and analytically counts the
exact abstract Toffoli/CCX and controlled-SWAP primitives generated by that
construction.

No QPU service was contacted.

## Cases and guardrails

All three previously established scout cases passed the same public
repeated-squaring/GCD classical-shortcut guardrail for both baseline and
transformed bases.

| N | ell | base -> transformed | validation order |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 209 | 3 | 3 -> 27 | 90 -> 30 |
| 713 | 5 | 19 -> 563 | 330 -> 66 |
| 781 | 7 | 29 -> 380 | 70 -> 10 |

The staged policy uses 4,6,...,`2*bit_length(N)` phase bits with four shots per
stage and the same conservative nearest-bin strict-verification model used by
the earlier Dark Star audits.

## Main result

The Dark Star stopping benefit survives the switch from exponential
full-register truth-table synthesis to a polynomial reversible-arithmetic
resource model.

| N | baseline phase rounds | transformed phase rounds | phase-work reduction | baseline expected CCX | transformed expected CCX | CCX reduction |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 209 | 168.8405 | 95.8453 | **43.23%** | 2,198,155.9 | 1,249,683.7 | **43.15%** |
| 713 | 266.1742 | 134.7178 | **49.39%** | 7,429,533.1 | 3,806,858.9 | **48.76%** |
| 781 | 128.0546 | 39.0769 | **69.48%** | 3,665,786.1 | 1,124,238.3 | **69.33%** |

Success probability by the textbook precision cap also increased:

- N=209: 0.92854 -> 0.98717;
- N=713: 0.93527 -> 0.99728;
- N=781: 0.99884 -> 0.99998.

## Mechanism isolation

The decisive diagnostic is the transformed/baseline CCX ratio divided by the
transformed/baseline phase-round ratio.

| N | phase-round ratio | CCX ratio | per-phase-round CCX ratio |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 209 | 0.567667 | 0.568515 | **1.00149** |
| 713 | 0.506126 | 0.512395 | **1.01239** |
| 781 | 0.305158 | 0.306684 | **1.00500** |

This is an important correction to the interpretation of the earlier
IBM-target truth-table receipts.

Under the scalable arithmetic construction, the transformed modular constants
are not intrinsically cheaper. Per executed phase round they are actually about
0.15% to 1.24% *more* expensive in abstract CCX count.

Therefore the robust Dark Star resource mechanism is:

> **earlier adaptive stopping because the transformed order is smaller, not a
> lucky same-precision compiler simplification of the transformed base.**

The previous same-precision CZ reductions seen in the N=713/N=781
full-register compiler should therefore be treated as implementation-specific
secondary effects, not part of the general Dark Star claim.

## Recycled-width result under scalable arithmetic

The generic clean-ancilla arithmetic has a much larger coherent work footprint
than the earlier N=35 truth-table validation path, as it should.

For this construction:

- N=209 arithmetic workspace excluding phase: 27 qubits;
- N=713 and N=781: 33 qubits.

At the textbook phase cap:

| N | wide peak logical qubits | recycled peak logical qubits | reduction |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 209 | 43 | 28 | **34.88%** |
| 713 | 53 | 34 | **35.85%** |
| 781 | 53 | 34 | **35.85%** |

Dark Star does not change these peak-width numbers because it does not change
the textbook precision cap. Phase recycling is the mechanism that lowers
simultaneous width; Dark Star lowers the expected sequential work.

That separation is exactly what the combined experiment was intended to test.

## Combined interpretation

The two mechanisms survive together without needing to multiply or conflate
their headline percentages:

- **recycled QPE:** lowers simultaneous coherent width;
- **Dark Star:** lowers expected controlled modular-arithmetic work through
  earlier public-verification stopping;
- **generic modular arithmetic:** remains fully coherent and is explicitly
  retained in the width and Toffoli accounting.

For the three scout cases, the combined Dark-Star + recycled configuration
therefore uses about 35% fewer peak logical qubits than the matched staged wide
configuration while reducing expected generic-arithmetic CCX work by about
43%, 49%, and 69%, respectively, compared with the untransformed baseline.

The expected CCX reduction is the same for wide and recycled within a given
policy because both architectures execute the same controlled modular
multiplications; their distinction is phase-register width and QFT versus
measurement/reset/feed-forward overhead.

## Why this is stronger than the previous compiler receipts

The earlier N=209/713/781 studies established that the stopping effect survived
IBM-target compilation, but their exact full-register modular permutation
synthesis grows exponentially with work-register width.

This audit removes that particular objection. The arithmetic construction used
here grows polynomially with modulus width and does not use the hidden order or
reachable orbit.

It is not claimed to be an optimal fault-tolerant modular multiplier. The
absolute Toffoli counts are construction-specific. The important result is that
the *direction and approximate size of the stopping reduction* survive when the
arithmetic representation is changed to a scalable generic family.

## Claim boundary

Supported:

> In three preregistered small-semiprime scout cases that survive public
> classical-shortcut checks, residue-conditioned odd-power preconditioning
> reduces expected staged Shor modular-arithmetic work by roughly 43-69% under
> an explicit polynomial reversible-arithmetic resource model. Separately,
> iterative phase-register recycling reduces peak simultaneous logical width by
> roughly 35% for that arithmetic construction. The arithmetic-cost reduction
> is explained almost entirely by earlier stopping rather than cheaper
> transformed multipliers.

Not established:

- measured hardware runtime or fidelity improvement;
- fault-tolerant physical-qubit or surface-code cost;
- population-average savings for arbitrary semiprimes;
- an asymptotic improvement to Shor;
- practical RSA-2048 capability;
- novelty of the odd-power order identity itself.
